We introduce the Controlled Exposure Framework (CEF) to guide the design of AI-assisted workflows around three dimensions: how often people encounter AI outputs, how much cognitive work they offload, and whether AI replaces human reasoning. The framework connects these dimensions to three families of interventions: calibration cues, reflection and friction prompts, and exposure and access controls.

An exploratory within-subject study with 21 participants and 378 task decisions compared three interfaces for reviewing AI-generated summaries: minimal constraints, confidence indicators and verification reminders, and explicit confirmation with written justification. Participants corrected a larger share of erroneous summaries under moderate and high control (73.8% and 77.8%) than under low control (57.1%), while preserving almost all already-correct summaries. Participants most often selected high control for preventing incorrect acceptance, but most often preferred moderate control for organizational use.
